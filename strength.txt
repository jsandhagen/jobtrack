RESUME STRENGTH SCORE: RATIONALE
================================

Status (October 2026): in UAT (src/main/resumeStrength.js), and now the
optimizer's goal. ATS is a floor, not the target: see "Hiring manager first"
at the end. Optimize runs ~220-400 ms on the big-library bench (the strength
score caches its posting-side work and line comparisons).


WHY A SECOND SCORE
------------------
The goal of the one-click optimizer is the best resume for the posting, not
the best ATS number. Until now it maximized the ATS score and used relevance
only to break exact ties. The ATS score answers one question: will screening
software pass this page? A recruiter then spends a few seconds on the page and
asks a different one: do I want to talk to this person? Pages can pass the
first and fail the second:

  - The summary restated the first bullet ("Led competitive intelligence for
    the CTO." directly above "Led competitive intelligence for the CTO,
    tracking ..."), spending the most-read line on a repeat.
  - On a public-sector role the summary's proof was a banking sales play.
  - Bullets of near-equal ATS value were swapped by score alone, sometimes
    for the weaker one in a reader's eyes.

The fit score doesn't cover this either: it judges the person against the job
from all their documents, not how well one page presents them.

So the strength score measures the page as a recruiter screens it, and the
optimizer now aims at ATS and strength together.


WHAT IT MEASURES
----------------
A 0-1 score with a grade (A >= 0.85, B >= 0.72, C >= 0.58, else D), from four
parts. Parts that don't apply to a posting are left out and the rest
re-weighted.

1. Lead (35%): are the first two bullets of the latest role the strongest
   proof the person has for this posting? Compared with the two best bullets
   the page could carry, as ranked by the same bullet ranker the optimizer
   uses. A bullet that tells the same result as one already on the page
   doesn't count as available. Weighted most because the top of the latest
   role is what a skimming reader actually reads.

2. Must-haves shown (30%): the share of the posting's required
   qualifications that a bullet or the summary shows, read the way the fit
   score reads them. Degrees (Education shows them), the kind of experience
   asked for (the role shows it) and soft skills (nobody screens on them) are
   left out.

3. Summary (20%): three checks, multiplied together.
     - On topic: it names a skill, must-have or kind of work the posting is
       about. Industry words the optimizer appends for keyword searches
       ("Enterprise Software experience at Appian.") don't count. Off topic: 0.4.
     - Not an objective: "Looking for opportunities in ..." says what you
       want, not what you bring. 0.5.
     - No echo: restating the first bullet 0.4; the second bullet 0.75.
   Sector: if the posting is set in a sector (public sector, financial
   services, healthcare) and the page has proof from it, that proof should be
   in the summary or the first two bullets. Otherwise 0.7.

4. Results (15%): the share of bullets with a number or a scale, full credit
   at 60%. Weighted least because the optimizer can't add numbers that aren't
   in the person's documents.

Two bullets that tell the same accomplishment cost 15% of the total. The
optimizer already never does this; the check is there for hand edits.

The score also returns plain-language notes ("The summary repeats your first
bullet.") for the editor to show.


HOW THE OPTIMIZER USES IT
-------------------------
Each candidate arrangement of the page is valued at:

    ATS score + 20 x strength

So a 0.05 gain in strength (a summary that stops echoing the first bullet) is
worth about one ATS point. The weight is set so neither can be bought cheaply
with the other: a change that costs several ATS points must buy a clearly
better read, and a small strength gain can't sink a real ATS gain.

The summary generator was changed to aim at the same things:
  - Its proof sentence is never the first bullet. It prefers an accomplishment
    from further down the page, then the second bullet.
  - It prefers proof from the posting's sector.
  - The proof must be strong evidence for this posting: at least about a
    third as relevant as the best bullet on the page, and a clause that says
    something (seven words or a result; "Advised product and GTM leaders" is
    not enough).
  - With nothing that strong (one bullet carries the role), the summary names
    the kinds of work and stops, rather than restate the first bullet. This
    reverses an older rule that the summary always quotes an accomplishment.
  - Off-page proof that tells the same result as a bullet on the page is
    never used.


WHAT IT CAN'T JUDGE
-------------------
  - Whether an achievement is impressive for this employer, or a number right.
  - Gaps in the person's documents. A page can grade A against a posting the
    person isn't qualified for; that's the fit score's job.
  - Taste. The bullet ranker decides which bullet is "strongest", and it can
    disagree with a reader. Example: for a CI role it prefers "Built analytical
    reports and dashboards ..." over "Translated technical concepts into
    executive-ready insights ...".
  - It never writes anything. Rewording, merging two bullets that tell one
    result, or adding a documented passage stays with the person (the
    optimizer's notes and "Ask if applicable" say which) or the Claude path.


EVIDENCE SO FAR
---------------
Calibrated on the fictional e2e candidate (test/e2e/fixtures) against 16
good-fit postings: the 13 live postings saved in
test/fixtures/allianceOpportunities.js (on UAT, with fit bands in
test/fitAllianceRoles.test.js and ATS bands in test/atsApplied.test.js) plus
3 from test/fixtures/randomJobs*.js.

Before the summary changes, 4 of 16 pages had a summary that repeated the
first bullet (Salesforce PubSec CI, EvenUp CI, Mark43 CI, AWS SAP alliances),
and one public-sector page had a summary with no public-sector proof. After
them, none repeat the first bullet and every page grades A or B. The pages
still marked down are:
  - Supply Chain Analyst and Financial Analyst: the person's own summary is
    about their CI and partnerships work, not the posting's. The one generated
    alternative is a template, so the honest signal is the note.
  - Weyerhaeuser and A-LIGN: the pages show only 20-40% of the must-haves
    (wood products industry; M&A execution), because the documents don't.

Open items before release:
  1. A second candidate in another field, to check the weights and grade
     edges hold beyond this persona.
  2. An independent judge: pairwise "which would you interview?" comparisons
     (a person, or Claude with a hiring-manager rubric) on the saved
     postings, to check this score agrees with a reader rather than with
     itself.
(Done: caching for speed; tests in test/resumeStrength.test.js; the
strength grade in test/e2e/pipeline.js.)


HIRING MANAGER FIRST (October 2026)
-----------------------------------
The optimizer now values a page at

    100 x strength + 0.5 x ATS - 5 x (points below the floor)

with the floor at 75 (or where the page started, if lower). Above the floor
0.01 of strength is worth 2 ATS points; below it each point costs a lot.

The strength score also checks, in its parts:
  - Lead: the first bullet is the work the job is titled for (CI for a CI
    manager) when the role shows it (x0.75 if not); it isn't a supporting
    verb ("Collaborated", "Provided": x0.8, second bullet x0.9); a senior
    posting's first two show ownership or scale (x0.85). Proof from the
    posting's sector counts as top-ranked ("deals with the Army, Navy and
    DARPA" for a public sector role).
  - Summary: repeating any bullet costs (first 0.4, second 0.75, others
    0.8); a proof sentence outside the posting's kind of work 0.8; years that
    disagree with the role dates (5 claimed, 7 dated) 0.75, overclaiming 0.6.

And the optimizer:
  - Tries every pair for the latest role's first two bullets (the ranker
    orders the rest).
  - Writes the summary's proof only from an accomplishment off the page, in
    the posting's kind of work or sector; otherwise names the kinds of work
    and stops. No more "SaaS experience at Appian." sentences.
  - Offers your own summary with its years brought up to the role dates.
  - Lets draft bullets from your other documents (the ones "Ask if
    applicable" offers, good fits only, none that are thin) compete for the
    page. One that makes it carries a flag to check before sending, and
    isn't in the bank until saved.
  - Suggests a merged bullet (Use / Dismiss in the editor) when two bank
    bullets tell one result and the one left out adds ATS or is among the
    strongest for the posting; built only from the two bullets' own words.
  - Claude's drafts, when trimmed to fit, keep the first two bullets of the
    latest role, and a rewritten bullet is valued by its new words.

Run on 18 saved postings (13 earlier + 5 new good fits: Okta, LivePerson,
Hightouch, Zip, Palo Alto Networks), mean ATS 79.3 -> 80.3 and strength 0.82
(main) / 0.86 (previous UAT) -> 0.88.
